True wind conditions

  • TWS ranged from 1.1 to 16.0 kn (avg 9.4 kn; middle 90% was 5.8–12.7 kn) — consistent with the "light to moderate" conditions noted for this race.
  • Direction was the steadiest of the season logged so far: circular std of only 9°, holding in a 135–162° band the entire race (mean 144°) with no real shift, just gentle back-and-forth within that narrow range.
  • Wind built from 8.5 kn at the start to a mid-race peak of 13.1 kn (21:13 UTC), held in the high single digits to low teens for most of the race, then eased to 5.6–7.2 kn in the final 20 minutes.
  • Race duration (first underway sample to last maneuver, ~2.5 hours) and 24 total maneuvers (17 tacks + 7 gybes) are consistent with the "long course" noted for this race — the most maneuvers logged in any race except 2026-06-17's 27.

Tacks and gybes

  • 17 tacks — second-most of any race this season — avg duration 8.8 s, avg speed loss 36.7%, avg recovery 7.2 s.
  • Best tack: 22:29:50 — a 96.8° swing losing only 10.7%, the cleanest tack of the race. Worst: 21:27:11 — a slow 14 s tack with a wide 136° swing, losing 54.4%.
  • A notable sequence: three tacks in barely over a minute (22:29:50, 22:30:33, 22:30:57) produced wildly different outcomes — 10.7%, 47.8%, and 39.0% loss. Likely explanation: a rapid succession of tacks (a shift, a mark, or a tactical situation) where consistency broke down under time pressure.
  • 7 gybes, avg duration 9.3 s, avg speed loss 27.9% — fairly consistent execution, no major outliers. Best was 22:38:34 at 21.9% loss; slowest was 22:57:14 at 14 s.

Upwind performance

  • Beat boat speed averaged 68.4% of polar target (4,482 samples, avg TWS 9.4 kn).
  • Average TWA was 43.9°, about 4° wider than the polar-optimal 39.9° at this wind speed — a moderate gap.
  • Actual VMG averaged 2.90 kn vs. a polar target of 4.25 kn — 68.2% of target VMG, essentially matching the 68.4% boat-speed figure.
  • Likely explanation: since VMG% tracks boat-speed% almost exactly despite the 4° angle gap, the angle difference doesn't appear to be meaningfully costing VMG here — the shortfall reads as a raw boat-speed issue more than a pointing issue.

Power and boat handling

  • Average heel while beating was 14.6°, sailing with 3 crew in a 9.4 kn average breeze — reasonable for the conditions.
  • Rate of turn upwind averaged 1.37°/s, unremarkable relative to other races.
  • Data insufficient: no traveler/vang/rudder data available to assess power management directly.

Boat trim

  • Heel scaled down predictably by point of sail (14.6° beat → 4.8° reach → 4.3° run); no finer within-leg breakdown available.

Comparison with other races

  • Closest wind-speed match: Race 5 (9.2 kn beat avg vs. this race's 9.4 kn). Race 9 (8.4 kn, the most recent prior race) is also a reasonable comparison.
  • Vs. Race 5: this race had a tighter angle gap (4° vs. 7.3° wide of target) but lower relative boat speed (68.4% vs. 78.4%) — again pointing to speed rather than angle as this race's limiter.
  • Vs. Race 9 (same crew, similar wind): this race was notably faster relative to target (68.4% vs. 59.6%) and had a tighter angle gap (4° vs. ~4.6°), despite sailing more maneuvers (17 tacks vs. 14) — a positive sign that tacking volume didn't come at the cost of upwind performance this time.

Coaching priorities

  1. Tack consistency under pressure (high confidence). Three tacks within about a minute (22:29–22:31) ranged from 10.7% to 47.8% loss — the same crew, same conditions, wildly different outcomes. Action: review that sequence specifically; it's a clear example of what "good" (10.7%) and "bad" (47.8%) look like back-to-back.
  2. Raw upwind speed vs. target (medium confidence). Angle was reasonably close to optimal (4° gap) but boat speed was still only 68.4% of target. Action: since angle isn't the main issue here, focus on trim and speed-building in the 8–13 kn range rather than pointing higher.
  3. The single worst tack at 21:27:11 (low confidence — one data point). A 14 s tack with a 136° swing lost 54.4%, the worst of the race. Action: worth a look, but treat as an outlier rather than a pattern given the otherwise-solid tacking shown later in the race.

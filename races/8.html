True wind conditions

  • TWS ranged from 0.6 to 14.0 kn (avg 6.1 kn; middle 90% was 3.1–9.4 kn) — the lightest average of any race reviewed.
  • Direction drifted within a ~35° band: starting around 55–57°, backing to 22–23° through the middle of the race (21:13–21:23), then returning to 29–35° for the remainder — a moderate oscillation rather than a one-way trend (circular std 16°).
  • Wind built to a brief peak of 9.0 kn (21:30), then eased steadily and died out toward the finish, dropping to 3.2–3.5 kn in the final 15 minutes.

Tacks and gybes

  • 4 tacks — the smallest tack count of any race reviewed — avg duration 11.5 s, avg speed loss 31.8%, avg recovery 13.5 s (the slowest average recovery of any race in the dataset).
  • Best tack: 21:01:14 — tighter 86° swing, 21.8% loss. Worst: 21:45:44, in dying breeze near the finish — a 114° swing losing 45.2%, the worst single tack of this race.
  • 3 gybes, avg duration 11.3 s, avg speed loss 18.2% — fairly consistent, no standout issues.

Upwind performance

  • Beat boat speed averaged 58.8% of polar target (2,205 samples, avg TWS 6.5 kn) — the lowest relative boat speed of any race reviewed this season.
  • Average TWA was 43.6°, very close to the polar-optimal 42.5° at this wind speed — only 1.1° off, one of the tightest angle matches in the dataset.
  • Actual VMG averaged 2.31 kn vs. a polar target of 3.41 kn — 67.7% of target VMG, notably better than the 58.8% boat-speed figure, again consistent with the near-perfect angle.
  • Likely explanation: with angle essentially matching target, this race is the clearest example yet that raw boat speed — not pointing or tacking technique — was the dominant limiter. Very light, dying wind for parts of the race likely compounded the shortfall.

Power and boat handling

  • Average heel while beating was 9.7°, consistent with the very light 6.5 kn average wind.
  • Rate of turn upwind averaged 1.10°/s, unremarkable.
  • Data insufficient: no traveler/vang/rudder data available to assess power management directly; in this light a well-trimmed boat and an undertrimmed one can look similar on heel/rate-of-turn alone.

Boat trim

  • Reach performance was also the weakest of the season here (46.0% of target, 691 samples), alongside the weak beat number — consistent with an overall slow night rather than an isolated upwind issue.

Comparison with other races

  • Four other races were sailed in broadly similar (light-to-moderate) wind: Race IDs 4 (8.2 kn beat avg), 5 (9.2 kn), 6 (7.5 kn), and 7 (7.0 kn).
  • This race had the lowest relative boat speed of the whole season (58.8%), despite one of the tightest angle matches to target (1.1° off) — reinforcing, more strongly than any other race, that raw speed rather than angle or tacking was the limiting factor.
  • Tack execution (31.8% avg loss) was mid-pack — better than Races 4 (36.9%) and 5 (35.6%), worse than Races 6 (34.0%) and 7 (23.5%) — but on only 4 tacks, a small sample.

Coaching priorities

  1. Raw boat speed in very light air (high confidence). This was the season's slowest night relative to target (58.8%) despite a near-perfect upwind angle. Action: in sub-7 kn conditions, prioritize light-air speed technique (crew weight forward/leeward, minimal helm, sail shape for light air) over angle or tacking drills — angle clearly isn't the issue here.
  2. Tack recovery in light/dying air (medium confidence). Average recovery took 13.5 s, the slowest of any race, and the worst single tack (45.2% loss) happened in the dying breeze near the finish. Action: in fading wind, prioritize keeping the boat moving through the tack over pointing high immediately afterward.
  3. Small sample size caveat (context, not an action item). Only 4 tacks and 3 gybes were logged this race — enough to spot patterns but not to draw firm conclusions about technique on their own.

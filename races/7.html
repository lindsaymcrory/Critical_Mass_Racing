Race Summary

Wind & Comparable Races

True wind ranged 0.1–12.7 kn (avg 6.6 kn) — a light, patchy night. This is the earliest race in the dataset, so there are no previous races to compare against; the numbers below stand alone as a baseline for future comparisons.

Speed made roughly two-thirds of target upwind (66.8%) and 71.1% downwind, with the reach well behind both at 52.2% — the softest point of sail by a wide margin. Upwind pointing was tight: average TWA of 43.7° was within 1.6° of the 42.1° target, so the upwind shortfall reads as mostly a speed gap rather than a sailing-angle gap — actual VMG (2.61 kn) reached about 73% of the 3.58 kn target VMG at the same wind. Downwind, TWA averaged 150.4° against a 146.0° target, a few degrees deeper than optimal.

Five tacks lost 23.5% of boat speed on average but recovered in just 3.6 s, and two gybes lost only 16.0%. No mark roundings were logged this race, so leg-by-leg gain/loss can't be isolated beyond the point-of-sail splits above. This race predates the site's gun-time anchoring added later in the season, so no start-of-race position or speed data is available. Heel while beating averaged 13.1° with little net side-to-side imbalance (signed average +3.0°), and pitch stayed close to level on all three points of sail — nothing in the trim data points clearly at the reach shortfall. The two clearest strengths this race were tack recovery time and upwind pointing angle; the reach is the most obvious place to find speed next time out.

  1. Reach mode is the weakest point of sail (52.2% of target, well below beat and run) — dedicate reach-specific speed/trim time separate from beat and run drills.

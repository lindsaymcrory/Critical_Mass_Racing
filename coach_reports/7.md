## True wind conditions

* This was a short race (~90 minutes logged). TWS ranged from **0.1 to 12.7 kn** (avg **6.6 kn**; middle 90% was 3.8–9.2 kn).
* Direction oscillated within a 190–240° band (circular std 20°, mean 219°) rather than trending cleanly one way — a genuine oscillating shift, not a steady veer or back.
* Wind built from 6.2 kn at the start to a peak of 9.0 kn (22:00 UTC), then eased through the second half, dropping to 3.6 kn briefly near the finish before recovering slightly.

## Tacks and gybes

* **5 tacks**, avg duration **10.4 s**, avg speed loss **23.5%** — the best average tack loss of any race reviewed — and avg recovery **3.6 s**, the fastest average recovery of any race reviewed.
* The result is a bit bimodal rather than uniformly good: three tacks were excellent (13.0%, 8.9%, 17.2% loss) and two were considerably worse (39.2%, 39.1%). Interestingly, the two best-executed tacks (21:54:59 and 22:20:33) both had *wide* heading swings (110° and 121°) with low loss — angle width alone didn't predict outcome here.
* **2 gybes**, avg duration **9.5 s**, avg speed loss **16.0%** — both clean, no standout issues.

## Upwind performance

* Beat boat speed averaged **66.8% of polar target** (1,821 samples, avg TWS 7.0 kn).
* Average TWA was **43.7°**, very close to the polar-optimal **42.1°** at this wind speed — only 1.6° off, one of the tightest angle matches reviewed.
* Actual VMG averaged **2.61 kn** vs. a polar target of **3.58 kn** — **72.9% of target VMG**, better than the 66.8% boat-speed figure, consistent with the near-perfect angle.
* **Likely explanation:** with angle this close to target and tacking execution the best of any race reviewed, the remaining gap to 100% target speed looks like a straightforward raw-speed shortfall rather than a technique or angle issue.

## Power and boat handling

* Average heel while beating was **13.1°**, broadly in line with other light-to-moderate wind races at similar strength.
* Rate of turn upwind averaged **1.31°/s** — unremarkable, mid-range for the dataset.
* **Data insufficient:** no traveler/vang/rudder data available to assess power management directly.

## Boat trim

* Reach performance this race was notably low (52.2% of target, though only 488 samples) — the weakest reaching performance of any race reviewed. No finer trim breakdown is available to explain why.

## Comparison with other races

* Four other races were sailed in broadly similar (light-to-moderate) wind: Race IDs 4 (8.2 kn beat avg), 5 (9.2 kn), 6 (7.5 kn), and 8 (6.5 kn).
* This race had **by far the best tack execution** of the group (23.5% avg loss vs. 31.8–36.9% for the others) and one of the tightest angle matches to target — yet its boat speed relative to target (66.8%) was still only mid-pack, better than only Race 8 (58.8%).
* **Season-level observation:** across nearly every race reviewed so far, raw boat speed relative to polar target — not tacking technique or upwind angle — has been the most consistent limiting factor. This race is a clean example: best-in-class tacking and a near-perfect angle, but still under 70% of target speed.

## Coaching priorities

1. **Raw boat speed vs. target (high confidence).** Angle (43.7° vs. 42.1° target) and tacking (23.5% avg loss, the best reviewed) were both strong, yet boat speed was still only 66.8% of target. *Action:* since technique here is already close to the boat's best, this points toward checking sail shape/trim or crew weight for genuine speed rather than more tacking or pointing drills.
2. **Tack inconsistency (medium confidence).** Two of five tacks lost ~39% while three lost under 20% — a real split rather than a consistent level. *Action:* review what was different about the two weaker tacks (21:56:29, 22:05:30) vs. the three clean ones.
3. **Reach performance (low confidence — small sample).** Reach boat speed was only 52.2% of target on just 488 samples, the weakest of any race reviewed. *Action:* worth a look, but the small sample size means this could be a short, unrepresentative stretch rather than a real pattern.

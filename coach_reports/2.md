*Data note: this race had a ~90-minute logger dropout (dead battery, ~19:00–20:29 UTC). Everything below reflects only the data from ~20:43 UTC onward — if the boat's early upwind legs or first maneuvers happened before the dropout, they're not captured here.*

## True wind conditions

* Across the logged portion, TWS ranged from **2.9 to 22.7 kn** (avg **13.1 kn**; middle 90% was 7.2–18.4 kn).
* Direction was the steadiest of the season so far: circular std of only **11°**, drifting slowly from ~336–351° early to 309–331° through the middle of the race, settling around 316–321° late — essentially a single, gentle left-hand trend, not an oscillating shift.
* Wind built through the first hour of logged data (8.6 kn at 20:43 up to a peak of 18.0 kn at 21:10), held in the mid-teens through most of the race, then eased steadily in the final 30–40 minutes down to 3.7 kn by the finish.

## Tacks and gybes

* **15 tacks**, avg duration **7.8 s** (faster than the 9.3 s seen in the comparable-wind race on 2026-07-13), avg speed loss **40.6%**, avg recovery **7.2 s**.
* The tightest, quickest tack of the race (21:25:30 — 5 s, 74° total heading swing) had by far the lowest loss at **18.9%**. The four widest-swing tacks (109–133° swings, at 21:05:58, 21:15:50, 21:18:45, 21:21:08) all lost **50–58%** of boat speed. **Observation:** tack quality tracked heading-swing width closely and consistently in this race — tight, quick tacks were clearly cheaper than wide, slow ones.
* Despite faster average tack duration than the 2026-07-13 race, average recovery time was slower (7.2 s vs. 4.5 s) — the turn itself was quicker, but getting back to full speed afterward took longer. **Likely explanation:** rushing the turn without a matching acceleration phase out of the tack.
* **2 gybes**: one clean (21:11:33, 7 s, 15.0% loss) and one in very light air near the finish (22:44:51, boat speed under 2.5 kn beforehand) that isn't a fair comparison point.

## Upwind performance

* Beat boat speed averaged **75.3% of polar target** (3,256 samples, avg TWS 13.8 kn), with average TWA of **43.2°** — much closer to the polar-optimal beat angle (**38.3°**) than the 2026-07-13 race's 51.1°.
* Actual VMG averaged **3.39 kn** vs. a polar target of **4.59 kn** — **73.9% of target VMG**, tracking closely with the 75.3% boat-speed figure.
* **Likely explanation:** unlike the 2026-07-13 race (where angle was the bigger drag on VMG), here the VMG shortfall is attributable fairly evenly to boat speed — the boat was pointing close to the right angle but simply not moving fast enough for it.

## Power and boat handling

* Average heel while beating was **20.3°** — essentially identical to the 2026-07-13 race (20.1°) at a similar wind strength, suggesting this is a repeatable pattern for the boat in the low-to-mid teens rather than a one-off.
* Rate of turn upwind averaged **1.69°/s**, noticeably lower than the 2.22°/s on 2026-07-13 despite similar wind — less active steering/correction this race. The data can't say whether that's calmer conditions, a steadier helm, or something else.
* **Data insufficient:** as with every race in this dataset, there's no traveler/vang/cunningham or rudder data logged, so power state (over/under/appropriately powered) can't be directly confirmed — the high, consistent heel figure is suggestive but not conclusive on its own.

## Boat trim

* Heel dropped sharply off the wind (20.3° beat → 9.1° reach → 3.1° run), the expected pattern; no finer trim breakdown is available to say more within the beat leg itself.
* **Data insufficient:** no per-maneuver or per-leg trim comparison is available from this summary to isolate trim's specific contribution to the speed shortfall.

## Comparison with other races

* Only **one** comparable race by wind speed: 2026-07-13 (avg beat TWS 13.5 kn vs. this race's 13.8 kn). Every other logged race averaged notably lighter air (6–10 kn) and is excluded from this comparison.
* This race sailed a **tighter** upwind angle (43.2° vs. 51.1°) but a **lower** % of target boat speed (75.3% vs. 82.7%) than 2026-07-13 — the same trade-off as noted from the other race's perspective, worth a crew discussion on footing vs. pointing in this wind range.
* Tacks were quicker in duration here (7.8 s vs. 9.3 s) and lost slightly less speed on average (40.6% vs. 42.9%), but took noticeably longer to recover full speed afterward (7.2 s vs. 4.5 s) — only 2 comparable-wind races are available, so treat this as a pattern to watch rather than a firm conclusion.

## Coaching priorities

1. **Tack recovery time (high confidence).** Tacks were quick to execute (7.8 s avg) but slow to get back to speed (7.2 s avg recovery, some as long as 19 s). *Action:* focus the next tacking drill specifically on the acceleration phase immediately after the boat comes out of the turn, not just the turn itself.
2. **Tack angle consistency (high confidence).** The tightest tack (74° swing) lost only 19%; the widest ones (109–133°) lost 50–58%. *Action:* aim to keep every tack's total heading swing under ~90° — the 74° tack is this race's own benchmark to repeat.
3. **Upwind boat speed vs. target (medium confidence).** Angle was close to optimal (43.2° vs. 38.3° target) but boat speed was still only 75.3% of target — the shortfall here looks more like raw speed than angle. *Action:* check crew weight placement and sail trim in the 13–14 kn range specifically, since angle wasn't the limiting factor this time.

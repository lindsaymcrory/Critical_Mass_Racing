## True wind conditions

* Across the logged portions, TWS ranged from **0.0 to 24.0 kn** (avg **10.0 kn**; middle 90% was 2.0–17.6 kn) — the widest range of any race reviewed.
* Direction moved a lot over the session: circular std of **45°**, the highest of any race so far. Early on (light air, ~130–150°) it swung through to 200–250° by mid-to-late race — a genuine large shift, though the earliest readings (under 3 kn TWS) are less trustworthy.
* Wind built steadily through the first two hours to a peak around 19–20 kn (18:00–18:20 UTC), then after the data gap settled into a more moderate 6–13 kn for the rest of the race, easing to near-calm (3–5 kn) by the finish.

## Tacks and gybes

* **19 tacks**, avg duration **10.7 s**, avg speed loss **36.9%** (the best average loss of any race reviewed), avg recovery **7.9 s**.
* Unlike the higher-wind races, tack quality didn't track heading-swing width cleanly here — some wide-swing tacks (e.g. 21:20:51, 115° swing) lost only **13.9%**, while a moderate-swing tack (22:11:16, 79° swing) lost **71.5%**, the single worst tack of the race, with boat speed dropping from 4.9 kn to 1.4 kn. **Likely explanation:** that one looks like a stalled/caught-in-irons tack rather than an angle problem — worth reviewing specifically rather than generalizing from it.
* **8 gybes**, avg duration **10.4 s**, avg speed loss **23.7%**. The first gybe of the race (17:11:46, in 15–16 kn breeze) was notably slow at **20 s** and lost **48.9%** — the slowest, costliest gybe logged all season.

## Upwind performance

* Beat boat speed averaged **70.4% of polar target** (8,843 samples — by far the largest sample size of any race — avg TWS 8.2 kn).
* Average TWA was **37.4°**, essentially matching (very slightly tighter than) the polar-optimal angle of **41.1°** at this wind speed.
* Actual VMG averaged **3.05 kn** vs. a polar target of **3.99 kn** — **76.4% of target VMG**, slightly *better* than the 70.4% boat-speed figure.
* **Likely explanation:** with angle essentially on target, the VMG shortfall here is squarely a raw boat-speed issue, not a pointing/angle issue.

## Power and boat handling

* Average heel while beating was **8.1°** — much lower than the higher-wind races (20°+), consistent with the lighter average wind (8.2 kn vs. 13–14 kn) rather than any change in technique.
* Rate of turn upwind averaged **1.22°/s**, the lowest (calmest) of any race reviewed, in keeping with generally lighter, steadier conditions for most of the session.
* **Data insufficient:** no traveler/vang/rudder data is available to say whether power was managed well in the higher-wind opening hour (up to 19.9 kn) before the gap.

## Boat trim

* Heel and pitch scale down predictably with point of sail and wind strength, as in other races; no finer within-leg trim breakdown is available in this summary.

## Comparison with other races

* Five other races were sailed in broadly similar (light-to-moderate) wind: Race IDs 5 (9.2 kn beat avg), 6 (7.5 kn), 7 (7.0 kn), and 8 (6.5 kn) all fall within a few knots of this race's 8.2 kn beat average.
* Within that group, this race had the **tightest** upwind angle (37.4°, essentially on the polar target) but was **not the fastest** relative to target — Race 5 (78.4%) and this race's own 70.4% both beat Race 6 (67.5%) and Race 7 (66.8%), while Race 8 (58.8%, the lightest air of the group) was slowest.
* **Race 7's tacks lost much less speed on average (23.5%) than this race's tacks (36.9%)** despite similar wind strength. Race 7 only logged 5 tacks vs. this race's 19, so treat this as a pattern to test rather than a firm conclusion — but it's worth a look at what was different about technique or conditions that night.

## Coaching priorities

1. **Upwind boat speed vs. target (high confidence).** Angle was essentially on target (37.4° vs. 41.1° optimal) but boat speed was only 70.4% of target and VMG 76.4% — the gap here is speed, not angle. *Action:* in this wind range (7–9 kn), focus on speed-building fundamentals — trim and crew weight — rather than pointing higher.
2. **The 71.5%-loss tack at 22:11:16 (medium confidence).** This single tack lost far more speed than any other logged this season, on a fairly ordinary 79° heading swing. *Action:* review this specific moment with the crew — it reads like a stalled/missed-timing tack rather than a technique pattern.
3. **Tack execution vs. Race 7 (low confidence — small comparison sample).** Race 7, in similar wind, averaged 23.5% loss per tack vs. this race's 36.9%. *Action:* worth a debrief comparing what felt different between the two nights, but don't over-index on 5 tacks vs. 19.

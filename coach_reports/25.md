### A note on this race's data

The boat's through-water speed sensor recorded 0.0 kn all race, and the wind numbers on this page are **apparent wind, not true wind** (true wind can't be calculated without boat speed). Everything below is therefore reconstructed: boat speed comes from the Vakaros unit's GPS speed over ground, and true wind is recalculated from the recorded apparent wind and that GPS speed. Speed over ground can include a small current effect that through-water speed wouldn't, and any error in it carries into the true wind and angle figures, so treat the numbers as good estimates rather than exact readings. The comparable race below used a working paddle wheel, so the comparison isn't perfectly like-for-like. Tacks vs. gybes are inferred from the reconstructed wind angle at each Vakaros-detected turn.

### Coaching Recommendations

1. Upwind the boat sailed about 8.6° wider than the target angle but still reached 74.8% of target speed — a strong number for the conditions, but that much footing costs height; in a fading breeze, a slightly higher groove may hold VMG better as the pressure drops.
2. The wind faded steadily through the race and speed fell with it (4.6 kn to 3.4 kn to 2.3 kn at the end). Getting to the pressure early, and being clear on when to call it, matters more than fine-tuning trim on a night like this — retiring once the boat was making 2 kn was a reasonable call.
3. Twelve tacks and nine gybes is a busy race for two people; the tacks held up well (34% average loss, better than race 12), and the gybes were clean at 27%. The 87-second turn is worth a look on the video or track to see what happened there, since it's the one maneuver that doesn't fit the pattern.

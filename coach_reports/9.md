## True wind conditions

* TWS ranged from **1.5 to 15.5 kn** (avg **8.4 kn**; middle 90% was 5.6–11.2 kn).
* Direction was fairly steady (circular std 13°, mean 155°), starting around 190° and settling into a consistent 150–165° band for most of the race, then backing further to 124–145° in the final 30–40 minutes — a real late-race left-hand shift.
* Wind built from 5.7 kn at the start to a mid-race peak of 11.3 kn (22:30 UTC), staying in the high single digits to low teens for most of the race, then easing to 5.3–6.5 kn near the finish.

## Tacks and gybes

* **14 tacks**, avg duration **9.1 s**, avg speed loss **38.6%**, avg recovery **5.8 s**.
* The two worst tacks of the race came early: **20:58:51** (19 s, an unusually wide 176° heading swing, **74.6%** loss — the worst single tack logged all season) and the very first maneuver, a rounding-turn at 20:53:25 with 62.2% loss. Both happened in the opening 15 minutes, before the boat settled in.
* After that rough start, tacks improved noticeably — several later tacks (21:35:24, 21:54:49, 22:00:03, 22:12:37, 22:25:56) all lost under 34%, with three under 7 s in duration.
* **2 gybes**, avg duration **16.5 s** (the slowest average gybe duration of any race reviewed) and avg speed loss **35.7%** — both happened close together (21:19:51, 21:21:36) in a light patch (~2 kn boat speed beforehand), which likely explains the high loss and slow execution.

## Upwind performance

* Beat boat speed averaged **59.6% of polar target** (4,046 samples, avg TWS 8.4 kn) — one of the lower relative-speed nights of the season.
* Average TWA was **46.5°**, about 5.6° wider than the polar-optimal **40.9°** at this wind speed.
* Actual VMG averaged **2.37 kn** vs. a polar target of **4.03 kn** — only **58.8% of target VMG**, essentially matching the 59.6% boat-speed figure.
* **Likely explanation:** with both boat speed and VMG percentages this close together, and angle moderately wide, both speed and angle look like contributors here rather than one dominant cause — though the very rough first 15 minutes (two costly maneuvers back to back) likely dragged down the whole race's average.

## Power and boat handling

* Average heel while beating was **10.1°**, consistent with the moderate 8.4 kn average wind.
* Rate of turn upwind averaged **1.03°/s**, unremarkable and in line with other races at similar wind strength.
* **Data insufficient:** no traveler/vang/rudder data available to assess power management directly.

## Boat trim

* Heel scaled down predictably by point of sail (10.1° beat → 3.4° reach → 1.1° run); no finer within-leg breakdown available.

## Comparison with other races

* The closest wind-speed match is Race 5 (9.2 kn beat avg vs. this race's 8.4 kn); Races 4 (8.2 kn), 6 (7.5 kn), 7 (7.0 kn), and 8 (6.5 kn) are also broadly comparable light-to-moderate-wind nights.
* This race's boat speed relative to target (59.6%) was among the weakest of the comparable group — only Race 8 (58.8%) was lower. Its angle (46.5° vs. 40.9° target, a 5.6° gap) was also wider than most of the comparable races (Races 4, 6, and 7 all matched target angle within 3.7°).
* The worst single tack of the entire season (74.6% loss, 20:58:51) happened in this race, in the opening minutes — worth treating as a rough-start outlier rather than representative of the crew's typical tacking, since performance clearly improved as the race went on.

## Coaching priorities

1. **Slow start to the race (high confidence).** The two costliest maneuvers of the entire season (a 62.2%-loss rounding-turn and a 74.6%-loss tack) both happened in the first 15 minutes, before later tacks settled into a much better pattern (several under 34% loss). *Action:* review what was happening in those first two maneuvers specifically — sail setup, starting-line traffic, or simply not yet in rhythm are all plausible and worth discussing with the crew.
2. **Upwind angle and speed both slightly off target (medium confidence).** TWA was 5.6° wide of optimal and boat speed only 59.6% of target — a combined shortfall rather than one dominant cause. *Action:* focus on both angle and speed together in this wind range (8–9 kn), rather than assuming one fix addresses everything.
3. **Gybe execution in light patches (low confidence — only 2 gybes).** Both gybes happened in a light-air moment (~2 kn boat speed) and were slow (avg 16.5 s) with high loss (35.7%). *Action:* worth a light-air gybe drill, but treat this as a single data point, not a season-long pattern.

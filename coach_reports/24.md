### A note on this race's data

The boat's through-water speed sensor didn't record anything this race (stuck at 0.0 kn all night), so the usual on-board maneuver/polar analysis is empty. This report instead reconstructs performance by combining the Vakaros unit's GPS track (position, speed over ground, heel) with the boat's own wind instruments (TWA/TWS, which recorded normally throughout) — cross-referenced second by second. Speed over ground can include a small current effect that through-water speed wouldn't, though Bedford Basin's current is normally minor. Tacks vs. gybes below are inferred from wind angle at each Vakaros-detected turn rather than read directly off a tack-side sensor.

### Wind & Comparable Races

True wind ranged 0.1–14.1 kn (avg 6.6 kn) — light and, per the race note, genuinely patchy ("the basin was a maze of different winds"). The closest comparable is race 8 (2025-09-24, Wed Nights) — a near-exact match on both wind (0.6–14.0 kn, avg 6.5 kn) and crew size (2-up, same as tonight).

Upwind the boat achieved 58.0% of polar target — essentially identical to race 8's 58.8%. The reach was the standout: 77.7% of target vs. race 8's 46.0%, a big gap in this boat's favour. Downwind came in lower at 51.3% vs. race 8's 67.4%, though that number rests on a thinner sample (fewer seconds spent on a run tonight). Both beat and run angles ran tight of target rather than wide: TWA averaged 7.8° narrower than the beat target and 10.2° narrower (higher) than the run target — a pinching pattern on both points of sail, the opposite of the wide/footing pattern seen in several other races this season. In light, shifting air like tonight's, that likely cost more than it gained.

Sixteen tacks were logged — a lot for a 1:44 race, consistent with a shifty night requiring constant response — averaging 7.2s duration and 29.6% speed loss, holding up about as well as race 8's 4 tacks (31.8% loss, though race 8's took longer at 11.5s). Only one gybe was logged and it was expensive (54% loss, 16s), against race 8's 3 gybes averaging 18.2% loss — too small a sample to read much into beyond "worth a second look." Four mark roundings were detected from the GPS track.

### Coaching Recommendations

1. Both beat and run tonight ran tight of target angle (7.8° and 10.2° respectively) rather than the wide/footing pattern seen elsewhere this season — in light, shifty air like tonight's, easing off a touch on both points of sail is likely worth more than pointing/depth.
2. The reach was clearly the strongest point of sail tonight (77.7% vs. a comparable race's 46.0%) — worth noting what was different in trim or angle, since it's a real outlier in a good direction.
3. Sixteen tacks in under two hours reflects a genuinely shifty night rather than a technique issue — average loss per tack (29.6%) held up fine under that volume; the one gybe was rough (54% loss) but it's a single data point, not a pattern yet.

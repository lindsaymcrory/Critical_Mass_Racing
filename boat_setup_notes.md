## Port vs. starboard

* Starboard tack is meaningfully faster on reaching angles (roughly 70°–150°) in light-to-moderate air (0–12 kn) — 0.3–0.7 kn faster in most buckets, in the best-sampled ranges (hundreds to over a thousand samples per bucket); port is faster upwind (20°–60°) in the 6–12 kn band.
* In 12–20 kn the pattern flips: port tack is faster almost everywhere, including the same reaching angles where starboard led in lighter air. A fixed rig-tuning bias should favor one side regardless of wind strength, so this reversal points more toward helm/trim/crew-position habits that vary tack to tack than toward an asymmetric rig.
* The 20+ kn band has too few samples (mostly single digits to tens per angle bucket, largely from one race) to draw any conclusion there yet.

## Hull condition

* No consistent decline in reaching speed (TWA 80°–100°) shows up across the season in any wind band — the two best-sampled bands (0–8 kn, 12–20 kn) hold steady or even improve from June into July 2026, the opposite of what bottom fouling would predict.
* Race-to-race swings (e.g. 2.35–3.70 kn in the 0–8 kn band) are larger than any trend, and more likely reflect wind-strength variation within that broad band than hull condition, since 0–8 kn spans everything from near-drifting to a solid breeze.
* The 2025 races and the 20+ kn band have too few comparable samples to include with confidence — there's no data-supported reason yet to suspect the bottom needs attention beyond the routine clean already logged on 2026-05-13.

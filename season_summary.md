## Season overview

* **Light air is where the boat gives up the most speed, by a wide margin.** Pooled across all races, boat speed as a percentage of polar target climbs steadily with wind strength: **60.3%** at 0–6 kn TWS, **66.6%** at 6–9 kn, **73.4%** at 9–12 kn, **79.2%** at 12–16 kn. That's a ~19-point gap between light and moderate air — the single most consistent pattern in the data.
* **Tacks cost roughly 1.6x what gybes do.** Season-wide: tacks lose **37.1%** of boat speed on average (106 logged); gybes lose **23.5%** (39 logged). Tacking is the bigger of the two maneuver gaps.
* **Reach is the weakest point of sail, season-wide** — not just in isolated races: beat averages 70.4% of target, run 69.0%, but reach only **63.6%**.
* **Heel correlates with performance even at a fixed wind speed.** Checked separately within the 6–9 kn and 9–12 kn bands (to rule out heel just tracking wind strength): in 9–12 kn, samples under 15° of heel averaged 62.8% of target vs. 87.3% at 25°+. This is consistent with being under-powered or under-trimmed at times — worth a crew conversation, not a hard rule, since sail force and heel can also be eased deliberately in gusts.
* **Mark roundings are a real, untracked opportunity.** 25 logged roundings lose **36.0%** of boat speed on average — about the same as tacks — but they're currently excluded from every coaching write-up (by design, since they're mark-influenced rather than pure technique).
* **Two things the data can't say yet:** there's no clean season-over-season improvement trend (tack loss swings 23–43% race to race, tracking each night's wind more than a practice curve), and crew-count effects (2/3/4 aboard) are confounded — only one race has a 2-person crew with usable data, and it happens to be the lightest-air night of the season, so that comparison isn't meaningful yet.

## Improvement priorities

1. **Light-air boat speed (high confidence).** The clearest, most consistent gap all season — roughly 19 points of target speed lost between the lightest and moderate wind bands. *Action:* dedicated light-air speed work (crew weight, sail shape, minimal helm) in the 0–9 kn range, where the gap is largest.
2. **Tacking technique (high confidence).** Tacks lose ~1.6x what gybes do (37.1% vs. 23.5%), and per-race reports repeatedly show the same pattern: tight, quick tacks lose far less speed than wide, slow ones. *Action:* drill roll-tacks aiming for a heading swing under ~90° and duration under ~8s, using each race's own best tacks as the benchmark.
3. **Reaching performance (medium-high confidence).** The weakest point of sail pooled across the whole season (63.6% vs. 69–70% for beat/run). *Action:* a dedicated look at reach trim and angle — this hasn't had focused attention the way upwind and downwind sailing has.
4. **Power and trim in light-to-moderate air (medium confidence).** Heel tracks performance even within a fixed wind band, suggesting the boat isn't always powered up to its potential. *Action:* check sheeting and power decisions specifically in 6–12 kn TWS, where the heel/performance link is clearest.
5. **Mark-rounding technique (medium confidence — new area, not yet analyzed in depth).** Roundings lose about as much speed as tacks (36.0%) but have never been broken down the way tacks and gybes have. *Action:* start tracking rounding entry angle, speed through the turn, and exit acceleration the same way tacks/gybes are tracked now.
